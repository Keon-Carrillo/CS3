**Scenario**

The PSHS school canteen is small and often gets crowded during lunch break. Students line up to buy food, but the process is slow because:

- Some students take too long to decide what to order.

- The cashier has to manually calculate totals and give change.

- There is no system to track which food items are running out.-

Your group’s task is to decompose this problem into smaller, manageable parts that could be solved with computational thinking (CT) Skills.


**Step 1: Identify the Big Problem**

- Main Problem: The system is too prone to human error and uses slow processes, thus making it inefficient and creating long lines.

**Step 2: Identify three to four Sub-Problems**

- Please list possible sub-problems:

  1. Customers can take some time to choose their order

  2. Math (computing change) is hard

  3. Easy to lose track on how many products are left

**Step 3: Define Computational Thinking Approaches**

- For each sub-problem, apply CT skills:

| Sub-problem | CT Skill | Solution/s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers can take some time to choose their order | Abstraction | -Display a menu featuring the food options with their prices. The user can choose as many foodstuffs as they want before totaling everything. |
| Math (computing change) is hard | Algorithm | -Each food option has a price. With each option added, its price gets combined, and the total cost will be displayed upon the user finishing their order. If they have extra money, it will also compute the difference/change. |
| Easy to lose track on how many products are left | Decomposition | -Each food option will be displayed with a number representing the amount. Choosing a food option will subtract 1 from the product count. |
